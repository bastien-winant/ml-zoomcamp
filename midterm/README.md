# Machine Learning Zoomcamp: Midterm Project
## Predicting the success of Himalayan climbing expeditions
### Introduction
This project builds a machine learning classifier that predicts whether a Himalayan climbing expedition will succeed
or fail, based on historical data from the Himalayan Database. The goal is to use past expedition records—such as
team characteristics, seasonal conditions, and logistics—to model the factors that contribute to successful summit attempts.

The binary classifier helps uncover patterns in high-altitude mountaineering that may not be immediately obvious and
provides a data-driven way to assess risk and expedition outcomes.

### Problem Description
Mountaineering expeditions in the Himalayas are extremely challenging due to unpredictable weather, altitude risks,
terrain difficulty, team experience, logistics, and many other factors. In addition, democratization of Himalayan
climbing expeditions has increased the amount of variance in climbers' level of experience at the start of such expeditions.

The rapidly changing landscape of Himalayan climbing has made old beliefs and conventions less applicable to new expeditions,
and involved parties need new tools for effective risk-assessment. Understanding what contributes to a successful summit
can be valuable for:
- Local authorities
- Climbers planning future expeditions
- Tour and guide companies
- Researchers studying high-altitude activities
- Insurance and risk assessment teams
- Sponsors funding expeditions

### Data
The Himalayan Database is a free online dataset that provides detailed historical records of climbing expeditions,
including:
- Mountain name and height
- Season and year
- Team size and nationality
- Leader and member experience
- Route and climbing style
- Success or failure outcome


Predicting expedition outcomes using machine learning allows us to quantify the relationships between these variables.
